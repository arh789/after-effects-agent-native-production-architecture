# Script And Slide Rules

Narration and slide text are parallel tracks:

```text
spoken narration = explanation, evidence, qualification, discovery
slide text       = the irreducible update the viewer must retain
```

One slide represents one logical move. It should name a state, expose a
dependency, show a contradiction, separate signal from noise, reconstruct a
model, lock in a result, or open the next question.

Generate a slide by asking:

```text
current state + pressure or contrast -> changed state
```

Do not divide narration into equal chunks, subtitle the voice-over, or force a
paragraph summary onto the screen. Keep one stable composition per slide unless
the user explicitly requests staged content.

Titles should carry the claim or operation. Body text should preserve only the
structural residue needed to understand a contrast, chain, stack, or procedure.
Use declarative compressed language and manual line breaks.

False branches may need to appear before they fail. Do not announce the final
insight before the argument earns it.

Use conventional bullets only when the list itself is the point. Otherwise use
stacked lines. A procedural slide may be dense when the procedure is evidence,
but its groups must remain legible on a phone.

Final test: before the slide the viewer believes X. After it, what must they now
believe, reject, distinguish, or predict? That answer is the slide.
